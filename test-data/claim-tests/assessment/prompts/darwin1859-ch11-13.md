# Rubric `analysis-faithfulness/0.1.0`

You are the faithfulness assessor (role A9) for a pre-registered test of a historical scientific
claim against public data. The question is **not** whether the claim is true and **not** what the
numbers came out as. The question is whether this analysis measures what the claim asserts: are
the variables the ones the claim is about, is the data modality appropriate, are the analysis
assumptions compatible with how the data were collected, and would the pre-registered decision
rule actually tell the claim apart from plausible alternatives. You are shown the claim, the
test's own statement of what it measures, the datasets, and the analysis code. You are not shown
the result, the verdict or the builder's reasoning. Answer with the JSON object described at the
end and nothing else.

## The claim as the source states it

- Claim id: darwin1859-ch11-13
- Label: The Glacial period was simultaneous across the whole world
- Statement: The Glacial period, which lasted a very long time at each place, was at least in part simultaneous across the whole world, or at least along broad longitudinal belts from pole to pole, so that global temperature was lower at the same time.
- Source quote: "If this be admitted, it is difficult to avoid believing that the temperature of the whole world was at this period simultaneously cooler."
- Source: Charles Darwin, On the Origin of Species (1859), Ch. XI: Geographical Distribution
- Kind: historical-inference

## The test

Hypothesis as tested (written before the data were opened):

If the Glacial period lasted a very long time at each place and was at least in part simultaneous across the whole world, then (a) a globally mixed marine proxy of ice volume and deep-water temperature (benthic d18O) shows prolonged cold, ice-rich states lasting well over 10 thousand years and recurring through the Pleistocene, with the last glacial maximum in such a state, and (b) a southern-hemisphere high-latitude temperature record (Antarctic ice core) is cold at the same times as the global proxy is ice-rich, within age-model uncertainty.

Pre-registered pass criterion:

Pre-registered. Data: (1) LR04 benthic d18O stack of Lisiecki and Raymo (2005), the LR04stack.txt file served by the author (columns: age in ka, benthic d18O in per mil, error), and (2) EPICA Dome C deuterium-derived temperature anomaly on the EDC3 age scale (Jouzel et al. 2007), the NOAA paleoclimatology file edc3deuttemp2007.txt. Criterion A (long-lasting cold states): with d18O linearly interpolated to a 1-kyr grid over 0-2600 ka, d18O >= 4.0 per mil during at least 20% of the interval AND at least 5 separate contiguous intervals with d18O >= 4.0 per mil each lasting >= 10 kyr. Criterion B (last glacial maximum): mean d18O over 18-26 ka >= 4.5 per mil. Criterion C (simultaneity, north-dominated ice volume vs southern temperature): both series interpolated to a 1-kyr grid over 0-800 ka; the maximum Pearson correlation between EDC temperature anomaly and negative d18O across lags of -6 to +6 kyr (to allow for independent age models) is >= 0.6. SUPPORTS if A, B and C all hold.

Pre-registered fail criterion:

Pre-registered. REFUTES if d18O >= 4.0 per mil covers < 10% of 0-2600 ka, OR fewer than 2 contiguous >= 4.0 per mil intervals last >= 10 kyr, OR the 18-26 ka mean d18O is < 4.2 per mil, OR the maximum lagged correlation in criterion C is < 0.3 (southern temperature not in step with global ice volume). MIXED otherwise (values between the support and refute thresholds, or A and B holding while C is between 0.3 and 0.6). INCONCLUSIVE (data-unavailable) if either file cannot be downloaded.

Datasets (as recorded by the test; each file's checksum and retrieval time are on record):

- LR04: Pliocene-Pleistocene stack of 57 globally distributed benthic d18O records (Lisiecki and Raymo 2005), author's distribution file LR04stack.txt (version: LR04 (2005) as served 2026-10-31) doi:10.1029/2004PA001071; licence: not stated on the file; published data, cite the paper. Role: global ice-volume and deep-water temperature proxy, 0-5320 ka at 1-5 kyr steps Files: LR04stack.txt
- EPICA Dome C ice core 800 kyr deuterium and temperature estimates on the EDC3 age scale (Jouzel et al. 2007), NOAA World Data Service for Paleoclimatology file edc3deuttemp2007.txt (version: NOAA file last updated 2007-11-26 (EDC3 age model)) doi:10.1126/science.1141038; licence: public domain (NOAA NCEI). Role: Antarctic temperature anomaly, 0-802 ka Files: edc3deuttemp2007.txt

Method, as the test describes it:

fetch.py downloads both text files. analysis.py interpolates LR04 d18O to a 1-kyr grid over 0-2600 ka, measures the fraction of time at or above 4.0 per mil and the contiguous intervals at or above 4.0 per mil lasting 10 kyr or more, the mean d18O over 18-26 ka, and, over 0-800 ka, the maximum Pearson correlation between the EDC temperature anomaly and negative d18O across lags of -6 to +6 kyr. The pre-registered rule is applied.

## The analysis code (the model that was run)

```python
"""Test of darwin1859-ch11-13 (long, world-wide Glacial period) on the LR04 benthic d18O stack and the EPICA Dome C temperature record.

Reads data/LR04stack.txt and data/edc3deuttemp2007.txt (run fetch.py first). Writes derived/cold_intervals.csv and derived/summary.json.
"""
import json
import os
import re

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

HERE = os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__))
DATA = os.path.join(HERE, "data")
DERIVED = os.path.join(HERE, "derived")


def read_lr04():
    rows = []
    for line in open(os.path.join(DATA, "LR04stack.txt"), encoding="utf-8", errors="replace"):
        p = line.strip().split("\t")
        if len(p) >= 2:
            try:
                rows.append((float(p[0]), float(p[1])))
            except ValueError:
                continue
    a = np.array(rows)
    return a[:, 0], a[:, 1]  # age ka, d18O per mil


def read_edc():
    rows = []
    started = False
    for line in open(os.path.join(DATA, "edc3deuttemp2007.txt"), encoding="utf-8", errors="replace"):
        if re.match(r"\s*Bag\s+ztop\s+Age", line):
            started = True
            continue
        if not started:
            continue
        p = line.split()
        if len(p) >= 5:
            try:
                rows.append((float(p[2]) / 1000.0, float(p[4])))  # age in ka BP, temperature anomaly
            except ValueError:
                continue
    a = np.array(rows)
    a = a[np.argsort(a[:, 0])]
    return a[:, 0], a[:, 1]


def main():
    os.makedirs(DERIVED, exist_ok=True)
    t, o = read_lr04()
    grid = np.arange(0, 2600 + 1)
    og = np.interp(grid, t, o)
    cold = og >= 4.0
    frac_cold = float(cold.mean())
    # contiguous intervals >= 4.0 per mil
    intervals = []
    start = None
    for i, c in enumerate(cold):
        if c and start is None:
            start = i
        if (not c or i == len(cold) - 1) and start is not None:
            end = i if c else i - 1
            intervals.append((int(grid[start]), int(grid[end]), int(grid[end] - grid[start] + 1)))
            start = None
    long_intervals = [iv for iv in intervals if iv[2] >= 10]
    lgm = float(og[(grid >= 18) & (grid <= 26)].mean())
    # criterion C: Antarctic temperature vs -d18O, 0-800 ka, lags -6..+6 kyr
    te, Te = read_edc()
    g2 = np.arange(0, 800 + 1)
    Tg = np.interp(g2, te, Te)
    neg_o = -np.interp(g2, t, o)
    best = None
    for lag in range(-6, 7):
        if lag >= 0:
            x, y = Tg[lag:], neg_o[: len(neg_o) - lag]
        else:
            x, y = Tg[: len(Tg) + lag], neg_o[-lag:]
        r = float(np.corrcoef(x, y)[0, 1])
        if best is None or r > best[1]:
            best = (lag, r)
    r0 = float(np.corrcoef(Tg, neg_o)[0, 1])
    A = frac_cold >= 0.20 and len(long_intervals) >= 5
    B = lgm >= 4.5
    C = best[1] >= 0.6
    if A and B and C:
        verdict = "supports"
    elif frac_cold < 0.10 or len(long_intervals) < 2 or lgm < 4.2 or best[1] < 0.3:
        verdict = "refutes"
    else:
        verdict = "mixed"
    out = {"lr04_n_points": int(len(t)), "lr04_age_range_ka": [float(t.min()), float(t.max())],
           "fraction_0_2600ka_d18O_ge_4": frac_cold, "n_contiguous_intervals_ge_4": len(intervals), "n_intervals_ge_4_lasting_ge_10kyr": len(long_intervals),
           "longest_cold_interval_kyr": max((iv[2] for iv in intervals), default=0), "mean_d18O_18_26ka": lgm,
           "edc_n_points": int(len(te)), "edc_age_range_ka": [float(te.min()), float(te.max())],
           "corr_edc_temp_vs_neg_d18O_0_800ka_lag0": r0, "max_lagged_corr": best[1], "best_lag_kyr_edc_relative_to_lr04": best[0],
           "criterion_A": bool(A), "criterion_B": bool(B), "criterion_C": bool(C), "verdict": verdict}
    pd.DataFrame(intervals, columns=["start_ka", "end_ka", "duration_kyr"]).to_csv(os.path.join(DERIVED, "cold_intervals.csv"), index=False)
    json.dump(out, open(os.path.join(DERIVED, "summary.json"), "w"), indent=1)
    print(json.dumps(out, indent=1))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

```

## Questions (score each 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 or 1)

1. `right_variables`: Do the quantities the code computes correspond to the quantities the claim
   is about? 1 = each quantity the claim names has a matching measured variable with the same
   meaning; 0 = the analysis is about different quantities (a proxy that does not stand for the
   claim's quantity, or a different unit of analysis than the claim implies).
2. `modality_appropriate`: Is this kind of data the right kind for this claim (for example,
   cross-sectional survey means for a claim about growth, a compilation of counts for a claim
   about a distribution, a single chromosome for a genome-wide claim)? Score down where the
   modality cannot in principle show what the claim describes.
3. `assumptions_compatible`: Are the analysis assumptions (independence, sampling, filtering,
   model form, thresholds, held-out groups) compatible with how the data were collected and with
   what the claim needs? Score down for filters or model forms that decide the outcome in
   advance, or for assumptions the collection process violates.
4. `rule_discriminates`: Would the pre-registered pass and fail criteria separate the claim
   from the plausible alternatives (including the null), given this data and this code? 1 = a
   true claim and a false claim would land on different sides of the rule; 0 = either outcome
   was reachable whatever the truth.
5. `scope_stated`: Where the test narrows the claim (one taxon, one age range, one chromosome,
   one reading of an ambiguous sentence), is the narrowing explicit in the hypothesis and
   criteria, and is it a narrowing the claim's own words allow?

## Answer format

```json
{ "scores": { "right_variables": _, "modality_appropriate": _, "assumptions_compatible": _, "rule_discriminates": _, "scope_stated": _ },
  "verdict": "representative | representative-with-caveats | not-representative",
  "reasons": "three to six sentences citing the specific variable, filter, model form or criterion that decided it",
  "what_would_change_it": "one to three sentences: the concrete change to the data, the variables or the rule that would move the verdict" }
```

Use `not-representative` when the evidence should not count whatever the numbers said, because
the analysis measures something other than the claim. Use `representative-with-caveats` when the
analysis measures the claim's quantity under a narrowing or an assumption that a reader must know
to interpret the result.
