Ranked by how close a precise, measurable claim already is to a proof inside an explicit model.

1. **W. D. Hamilton.** A social trait increases when \(rb > c\). Relatedness, benefit, and cost are estimable, and the rule predicts altruism, policing, and sex-ratio bias. Proved for inclusive fitness under weak selection and additive effects; outside that regime it stays empirical. Strongest caveat: strong selection and synergistic payoffs sit outside the proof, and there the inequality and the direction of evolution come apart.

2. **Motoo Kimura.** Most molecular substitutions are neutral, a neutral allele fixes with probability \(1/(2N)\), and divergence is clock-like. Site-frequency spectra, clocks, and \(d_N/d_S\) measure those predictions. Fixation probability and the diffusion approximation are theorems of the Wright–Fisher model; genome-wide predominance of neutrality remains open. Weak selection plus demography reproduces much of the same sequence pattern.

3. **R. A. Fisher.** The partial increase in mean fitness caused by selection equals the additive genetic variance in fitness. Breeding designs can separate that variance from the change in the mean. Proved in the Price–Ewens form, with fitness effects held fixed. That partial term is only one piece of the change in mean fitness; frequency dependence and a shifting environment move the total on their own.

4. **John Maynard Smith.** An evolutionarily stable strategy cannot be invaded by a rare mutant, and the Bishop–Cannings theorem sets equal payoffs on its support. Contest behavior, ownership conventions, and sex ratios are observable. Proved for infinite populations under replicator dynamics. Finite numbers, mutation, and genetic constraints leave actual attainment of the ESS open.

5. **Alan Turing.** Local activation plus faster-diffusing inhibition creates a spontaneous wavelength. Spacing, and how that spacing shifts when diffusion or production rates change, can be measured. Linear instability conditions are proved for reaction–diffusion equations; stable nonlinear patterns are proved in many models. Mechanical buckling and prepatterns yield similar forms, so a matching wavelength still leaves the mechanism open.

Charles Darwin is the deliberate omission: natural selection is the root idea, but a historical process rather than one closed statement that becomes a theorem under stated hypotheses.
