**Assistant:**

1. **George R. Price**
   - **Key Claim:** The Price Equation (\(\Delta \bar{z} = \text{Cov}(w, z)/\bar{w} + \text{E}(w \Delta z)/\bar{w}\)) partitions evolutionary change in any trait into selection and transmission fidelity.
   - **Testability:** Directly measurable whenever parent-offspring trait values and reproductive outputs are recorded in longitudinal datasets.
   - **Theorem Status:** **Already proved within a model**—it is an exact mathematical identity derived from the definitions of covariance and expectation over discrete mappings.
   - **Caveat:** Because it is a tautological identity, it lacks dynamical sufficiency and cannot predict future trajectories without extra assumptions about how fitness changes over time.

2. **Ronald A. Fisher**
   - **Key Claim:** The Fundamental Theorem of Natural Selection (FTNS): the rate of increase in mean fitness of a population at any instant equals its additive genetic variance in fitness at that instant.
   - **Testability:** Quantifiable via pedigreed populations or experimental evolution assays measuring narrow-sense heritability of fitness alongside partial mean fitness changes.
   - **Theorem Status:** **Already proved within a model**—strictly proved as a theorem (clarified by Price and Ewens) for the partial change in mean fitness due to allele frequency changes under constant environments.
   - **Caveat:** Total mean fitness often fails to increase in nature because epistasis, recombination, and deteriorating environments violate the partial-change isolation.

3. **Motoo Kimura**
   - **Key Claim:** Neutral Theory: at the molecular level, the fixation probability of a neutral allele is \(1/(2N_e)\), making the neutral substitution rate equal to the mutation rate (\(k = \mu\)) independent of population size.
   - **Testability:** Highly falsifiable via comparative genomics, molecular clock constancy across lineages of varying generation times, and site-frequency spectrum tests (e.g., McDonald–Kreitman).
   - **Theorem Status:** **Already proved within a model**—rigorously proved as an exact asymptotic theorem within Wright–Fisher diffusion and coalescent stochastic processes.
   - **Caveat:** Pervasive linked selection (background selection and genetic draft) in real genomes warps neutral genealogies, violating the independent-loci assumption.

4. **Alan M. Turing**
   - **Key Claim:** Reaction-diffusion morphogenesis: two homogeneously distributed morphogens with unequal diffusion rates (\(D_{\text{inhibitor}} > D_{\text{activator}}\)) can spontaneously break symmetry to form periodic spatial patterns.
   - **Testability:** Testable by perturbing morphogen diffusion/degradation rates in developmental systems (e.g., mouse digit patterning, zebrafish stripes) and checking if wavelength shifts match eigenvalue predictions.
   - **Theorem Status:** **Already proved within a model**—diffusion-driven (Turing) instability is a proved bifurcation theorem for coupled nonlinear PDEs.
   - **Caveat:** Classical Turing conditions are notoriously sensitive to parameter tuning and domain geometry, while real embryos rely heavily on mechanochemical feedback and gene-regulatory cascades.

5. **Geoffrey B. West (with James H. Brown & Brian J. Enquist)**
   - **Key Claim:** Metabolic rate scales with body mass as \(B \propto M^{3/4}\) (and biological times as \(M^{1/4}\)) because organisms minimize hydrodynamic/elastic energy dissipation through space-filling, self-similar fractal vascular networks.
   - **Testability:** Empirically testable across orders of magnitude via allometric regression of metabolic rates and 3D imaging of vascular branching ratios.
   - **Theorem Status:** **Provable** (partially proved within idealized geometric optimization models, while generalizations to heterogeneous networks remain **open**).
   - **Caveat:** Empirical exponents frequently deviate from \(3/4\) across clades, and organisms lacking fractal circulatory systems still exhibit near-quarter-power scaling.
