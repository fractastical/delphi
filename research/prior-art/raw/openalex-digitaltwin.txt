    "title": "From Biological Digital Twins to Embodied Digital Continuity: A Research Framework for Whole-Body Computational Modeling",
    "abstract": "Human digital twins are emerging as dynamic computational representations of individuals constructed from physiological, anatomical, behavioral, clinical, and environmental data. Current systems, however, are predominantly organ-specific, task-specific, or clinically bounded, and relatively few implementations satisfy strong definitions of a digital twin requiring personalization, dynamic updating, and predictive capability. This paper develops a research framework for extending biological digital-twin methods toward whole-body computational models capable of reproducing selected closed-loop dynamics relevant to embodied cognition. The framework follows from The Human Embodied Intelligence Research Program, in which full-body intelligence is treated as a proposed organism-level construct involving interactions among interoceptive, autonomic, neural, sensorimotor, and environmental processes. If those interactions prove empirically important, a computational representation intended to model embodied intelligence would require more than anatomical reconstruction or a static archive of biological measurements. It would need to represent internal states, update them dynamically, integrate multimodal signals, respond to perturbation, generate actions or predictions, and remain plastic under new conditions. Three engineering layers are distinguished: multimodal biological capture; dynamic computational embodiment; and longitudinal validation of fidelity, interaction, and plasticity. Contemporary digital-twin research, mechanistic physiological modeling, neuromechanical simulation, wearable sensing, and emerging interoceptive artificial-intelligence frameworks provide partial technological foundations for this agenda. They do not presently constitute a complete whole-body twin. The term embodied digital continuity is introduced as an operational research concept referring to persistence of validated subject-specific dynamical and behavioral properties across computational updating and interaction. It does not denote, and should not be interpreted as demonstrating, continuity of consciousness, preservation of personal identity, mind transfer, or literal survival. The scientific objective is therefore deliberately narrower than “uploading a person”: to determine which biologically validated dynamics can be reconstructed computationally, how reconstruction fidelity should be tested, which functions require closed-loop embodiment, and where biological-to-digital correspondence fails. This formulation transforms embodied digital continuity from a metaphysical assertion into a falsifiable engineering and modeling program.     "title": "A Phased Research Roadmap for Embodied Intelligence and Whole-Body Digital Twins: From Biological Measurement to Closed-Loop Computational Embodiment",
    "abstract": "The Human Embodied Intelligence Research Program proposes a staged research program connecting the empirical study of organism-level embodied cognition with the longer-term development of personalized, dynamically updated whole-body computational models. The program deliberately separates what can be tested now from what depends on unresolved biological findings or future technological capabilities. This paper translates the preceding theoretical, mechanistic, experimental, and technological manuscripts into a dependency-driven research roadmap. Rather than assuming that full-body intelligence constitutes a validated latent construct, that whole-body digital twins can presently be constructed, or that computational fidelity implies continuity of consciousness or personal identity, the roadmap treats each proposition as contingent on explicit empirical milestones. Three broad phases are proposed. Phase I establishes the biological and measurement foundation: validation of candidate dimensions of full-body intelligence, characterization of within-person and between-person variability, multimodal physiological acquisition, standardized perturbation paradigms, and initial personalized computational models. Phase II develops and tests closed-loop computational embodiment: multimodal integration, subject-specific state estimation, internal-state regulation, sensorimotor interaction, modality ablation, functional-equivalence testing, and increasingly rigorous digital-twin verification, validation, and uncertainty quantification. Phase III addresses longitudinal computational embodiment: adaptive updating, model drift, sustained subject-specific prediction, progressively richer environments, human–AI interaction, governance, and long-duration tests of computational continuity. Progression is governed by explicit go/no-go criteria. Failure to validate a unified full-body-intelligence construct does not terminate the program; it redirects modeling toward independently validated component mechanisms. Failure of anticipatory physiological activity does not undermine the mainstream embodied-cognition foundation. Failure of increasingly detailed bodily modeling to improve held-out prediction or adaptive behavior would count against strong versions of the embodied-digital hypothesis. Previously proposed 1–3-, 3–7-, and 7–15-year horizons are retained only as illustrative development scenarios, not forecasts. Scientific progress is instead defined by prerequisite completion and evidential thresholds. The resulting roadmap is designed to permit success, revision, branching, or termination at every stage. Its objective is not to guarantee eventual digital reproduction of a human being, but to determine empirically how far validated biological dynamics can be measured, modeled, reconstructed, coupled, and sustained computationally.
