✕ RETRACTED

A proposed gja5a link to fin length

Found by: our own check (the cell-cell subagent)

An earlier note from this thread offered Daane 2021's `gja5a` convergent substitution as a second link between the flying-fish genomics and the bioelectric fin-size story. The cell-cell subagent checked it and the link does not hold: the fin-length connexin in zebrafish is `gja1b` (cx43, the `short fin` locus), `gja5a` has no ZFIN fin expression or phenotype record, and Daane 2021's own text puts cx43 under *constrained*, not accelerated, evolution along the flying-fish branch. The `gja5a` substitution stays in the map's `fin_size` block as the paper's own data, and it is kept out of any causal chain. The real connexin tie to the potassium-channel story is `gja1b` with Seaver 2025, which is independent of Daane.

"Almost no molecular data exists for flying fish"

Found by: the project lead, reading a primary source the thread had not read

An annotated genome does exist: Xu et al. 2021 assembled 23,611 predicted protein-coding genes for Hirundichthys speculiger (mirrorwing flyingfish), of which 23,492 (99.5%) carry at least one database hit. What flying fish lack is expression and phenotype data, not genes.

TrEMBL alone accounts for 23,477 of the hits, and TrEMBL is automatically generated and unreviewed, so 99.5% is a measure of database coverage rather than of understood function.

✕ WITHDRAWN

"The TFF’s wings (aspect ratio 17.9) are more slender than any measured flying fish (3.0-16.9)"

Found by: our own parametric-model fix pass, reading Fish (1990) and Park & Choi (2010) in full

The two numbers used different definitions. 17.9 is span squared over the area of both pectoral fins (Park & Choi S^2/A1); Fish (1990) divides by pectoral plus pelvic fins plus the body between them. On Fish’s definition the TFF is at most 13.9, inside his range. What survives: 17.9 is 1.7 times the one measured specimen (10.68).

And the caveat that keeps it honest: The same pass found the adult flying-fish mass differs 2.2x between the one wind-tunnel specimen (0.060 kg) and the population length-weight fits (0.100-0.132 kg). The 9.9 m/s minimum flight speed holds only for the specimen; at population mass it is 12.7-14.6 m/s, still inside the quoted 10-20 m/s.

✕ CORRECTED

"Sustained aerobic powered flight is 29 to 38 times short; no teleost the measured record allows has an aerobic ceiling above 0.0458 W" (E3)

Found by: the studio engine, when a real zebrafish broke our own aerobic bound

Run on an unedited adult zebrafish, the studio flagged the fish red for swimming at speeds it was measured to sustain. The Fick ceiling (efficiency × oxycaloric × cardiac output × arterial O2) used a cardiac output borrowed from schoolmaster snapper, 58.48 ml/min/kg. A measured zebrafish at U_crit takes up 2630 mg O2/kg/h (Palstra et al. 2010, PLoS One 5:e14483, PMC3013094), 2.8 times the 950 the ceiling allowed; even its standard metabolic rate is 1.5 times over. The same proxies set the flying-fish numbers. With a zebrafish-derived O2 delivery (1600 mg/kg/h scaled to 60 g) and a skeletal-muscle efficiency (0.23, Gladman & Askew 2024) the envelope becomes 0.0833 W, and the flying-fish shortfall is about 10 to 30 times (central about 20), not 29 to 38. E3 is now version 2.0.0, scoped to its sources; version 1.0.0 is kept. What survives: sustained aerobic flight is still ruled out by an order of magnitude.

And the caveat that keeps it honest: No flying-fish respirometry exists in the project, so the correction is a cross-taxon transfer like the original proxies. No zebrafish cardiac output during exercise was found; which of cardiac output or blood O2 content is low is inferred through Fick. Every case keeps propulsive efficiency at 1 and the best L/D, so the real shortfall is larger than each figure.

One species states the annotation-versus-parameter gap better than any aggregate: a genome with essentially complete functional annotation and, in the paper that reports it, none of the eight parameters.

'Functionally annotated' here means at least one hit in at least one of four databases. TrEMBL alone supplies 23,477 of the 23,492, and a TrEMBL hit is a sequence-similarity match to an unreviewed entry, not a measured function. Annotation by homology is the weakest sense of the word.

simulation-readiness/readiness.json (worked_example)

The precedent, to size the task

The closest thing that exists to what is being asked: a model that predicts phenotype from genotype for every annotated gene of an organism.

  • 525 genes, in 28 sub-models — the life cycle of a SINGLE CELL; no anatomy, no tissues, no development
  • built from over 900 primary sources and more than 1,900 experimentally observed parameters
  • that is 3.62 parameters and 1.71 publications per gene

Not a prediction, just the same parameter density applied to a larger gene set, to size the task. Development would need more, not fewer, parameters per gene: a zygote-to-adult model adds cell states, positions, divisions and movements that a single bacterial cell does not have.

Six things the literature does not support in zebrafish

Each was proposed from tetrapod limb biology or assumed across fishes, and each was checked against the zebrafish evidence.

statusthe assumption, and what the evidence says
✕ not-establishedThe tetrapod AER/mesenchyme feedback loop runs on Fgf AND Wnt signals in both directions in the zebrafish pectoral fin.
The Fgf arms are supported in zebrafish, but the Wnt arm is explicitly NOT established there. Leerberg 2019 states that the Wnt-mediated induction of AER Fgfs known from chick and mouse 'has yet to be established in zebrafish'.

✕ not-establishedThe ZPA is the source of anteroposterior positional information in the zebrafish pectoral fin bud, as Shh is in the tetrapod limb.
Only partly. In shh-null sonic you mutants some aspects of anteroposterior polarity are set up WITHOUT Shh and only later depend on it for maintenance, so the zebrafish ZPA is not the sole source of anteroposterior information.
✕ refutedShh from the fin basal epidermis drives proliferation of the underlying skeletal progenitors during ray growth and regeneration.
Refuted for proliferation. Braunstein 2021 found no change in EdU+ intra-ray cells under Smoothened inhibition and states that earlier pro-proliferative conclusions may have been cyclopamine off-target effects…
✕ refutedwnt10a signalling maintains the pectoral fin fold (ZFA:0005317).
Shown for the MEDIAN fin fold, not the pectoral fin fold. wnt10a mutants collapse the median fin fold and fail adult unpaired fin metamorphosis; the abstract reports no pectoral fin fold phenotype, and the mechanism is loss of distal character (dlx2a, then col1a1a and other ECM genes) and actinotrichia positioning, not a named ligand acting on a receptor in…

It holds in zebrafish (met CRISPR null loses pectoral abductor muscle) but not across gnathostomes: in shark, Hgf and Met are not expressed at the right time or place for fin muscle precursor delamination and migration.

Slightly inverted, and not a test. Gliding halfbeaks, at a fin ratio of 0.274, carry 24 of the 38 derived states; flying fishes, at 0.687 — roughly 2.5× the fin ratio — carry 21.4. The count goes slightly down as the fin gets longer, so derived-state count does not scale with fin length.

  • A LADDER TO READ, NOT A TEST. Four groups (needlefish/saury, halfbeak, gliding halfbeak, flying fish) is far too few for any statistical claim.
  • THE NON-GLIDERS SCORE ZERO BY CONSTRUCTION. Sheet G lists only substitutions shared by gliders and ABSENT from outgroups, so non-gliding species carry the ancestral residue at these sites by definition; the zero is circular and is not a finding.
  • TRACKING IS NOT CAUSING. A residue that changes along with fin length across the series is consistent with involvement and is not evidence of causation.

And the scale of the edit, against natural divergence

The winged zebrafish differs from its reference by 2 edited loci and 1 amino acid. In just the 37 causal-chain proteins with medaka orthologues, zebrafish and medaka (same class, different orders) differ by 7300 amino-acid substitutions (16054 positions counting indels) (median identity 79.2%); zebrafish and a bat (Myotis) by 10205 substitutions in 33 proteins (median 74.0%); zebrafish and human by 10765 substitutions in 37 proteins. A handful of edits against thousands of differences in a few dozen proteins, and these are only the coding differences in a tiny gene sample: the genome-wide difference, coding and non-coding, was not measured here and is necessarily larger.

About the model. The "winged zebrafish" is 2 edited loci and 1 changed amino acid against the zebrafish reference protein set. That is a statement about a specified genotype, not a measurement of any animal that was built and weighed here.
