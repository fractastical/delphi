How a flying fish swims, taxis, takes off and glides, in equations

A point-mass model of one real fish, the darkedged-wing flying fish specimen whose fins Park and Choi measured in a wind tunnel, moving through five phases. Every parameter is listed below with where it came from; the ones that are assumed or tuned have sliders so you can see how much they matter. The sim does not know what a real glide looks like; the validation panel compares it with the published field figures, which are themselves second-hand. Nothing here uses bird data (class Aves is the frozen holdout). Scope: this is the organism level of a chain whose chemistry (gill exchange, haemoglobin), organ (cardiac output) and tissue (muscle power) levels are being written as separate sourced modules; until they land the power interface is a stub that returns zero and says so. Development (genome to morphology) and molecular dynamics are not simulated. Flying fish are not isometric within a species (Davenport 1992: fin area ∝ L1.83, wing loading ∝ L1.24), so this one 60 g specimen does not stand for the family; the designed flier, when it comes, changes parameters only, never these equations.

Parameters and their sources

The equations the integrator solves

State (x, z, vx, vz); z = 0 is the sea surface; integrated with classical RK4 at dt = 1 ms. Gravity: a_z += -g Air lift: L = ½ ρ_air v² S C_L(α) perpendicular to velocity (applies when the fins are out of the water) Air drag: D = ½ ρ_air v² S C_D(α) · f_ge(h/b) opposite to velocity C_L(α) = CL0 + (CLmax - CL0)·α/35° for α ≤ 35°, then falls linearly to 0 at 60° (stall; shape assumed) C_D(α) = CDmin + k·C_L², k from C_D(0°) = CL0 / (L/D)max f_ge(r) = 1 for r ≥ 0.97; linear to 0.86 at r = 0.33; 0.86 below (Park and Choi 2010, water surface; r = h/b, b = span/2) Water drag: D_w = ½ ρ_water v² A_f C_dw (submerged body); on the lobe only, scaled by the immersed fraction, while taxiing Thrust: T along the path; swim: T_swim = ½ ρ_water v_approach² A_f C_dw ; taxi: T_taxi = factor · T_swim while the lobe is in the water Path: the swim thrust acts along the assumed approach angle (the measured 20° leap pitch is a body angle, not the path); while taxiing the fish trims α each step so lift balances weight at half the lobe height with a damping term on the vertical speed (assumed control law, gain 2 per m and 2 s/m), bounded to [-10°, stall]; it leaves the water when even -10° gives more lift than weight (about 16 m/s with this polar) or at the take-off speed target, then holds the glide α. Buoyancy: taken equal to weight while submerged (assumed neutral). Powered flight: (hypothetical fish only) thrust along the path T = min(P_avail / v, 2W), P_avail = f_muscle · m · p_muscle − P_inertial, applied while airborne; the real fish gets none (it does not flap). Breach: the vertical velocity is set to zero when the body centre reaches the surface, on exit and on re-entry (shallow leap, tail dipped; assumed, no planing model) Phases: swim (z + lobe < 0) → taxi (lobe in water, body out) → glide (lobe clear) → taxi again on re-entry if v ≥ v_reentry, else stop.
