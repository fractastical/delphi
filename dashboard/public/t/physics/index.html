Is a flying fish inside Thompson's flight envelope?

In 1917 D'Arcy Thompson argued that a flier of fixed proportions must move at least at a speed that grows with the square root of its size, and that the work of flight grows faster than the muscle to do it. This page checks a real flying fish against that argument using only numbers we could trace to a fetched source, and reports what the published record does and does not contain. Everything here is read from results.json, status.json and the two CSV files beside this page; nothing is typed by hand.

The envelope

The lift a wing makes is ½ ρ v² S CL. To stay up it must equal the weight W, so the slowest possible lift-supported speed is vmin = √(2 (W/S) / (ρ CL,max)). That is Thompson's "must": speed grows as the square root of wing loading W/S, and under isometry as the square root of length. The dashed line below has that slope, drawn through the flying fish.

The blue line is the slowest speed at which the measured fins of a 0.06 kg darkedged-wing flying fish can carry its weight; the shaded band runs to three times that. Each bar is a speed from the glide table, coloured by how the source got it. Hover any bar or the band for the source, the locator and the retrieval time. The bridge: fin proportion from real specimens, under the envelope

The Genotype thread extracted Daane et al. 2021's measurements of 109 preserved beloniform specimens (Harvard MCZ): standard length and pectoral fin length, from needlefish through halfbeaks and gliding halfbeaks to flying fish. The same paper names the genes (lat4a with kcnh2a) that reproduce the flying-fish fin proportion in zebrafish. Here the five wind-tunnel specimens whose wing loading set vmin are placed on that series. Their fin length is not reported, only the tip-to-tip span and the dihedral angle, so each is an upper bound: fin length ≤ half span / cos(dihedral), because body width is at least zero. No length is converted to an area: there is no sourced shape factor, so none is assumed.

    What goes to the Lean thread

    The exponents are consequences of definitions, not measurements, so they belong in the proof checker. Eight statements, each with symbols, units, the assumptions that are not mathematics, and a mutant that must fail to compile, are in FORMAL-STATEMENTS.md. The one that matters most is strength scaling as L²: the Lean thread's open-problems ledger has 17 candidates it cannot rule on for lack of it.

    1. R1 cube law W ∝ L³ (already proved as thompson1917-ch03-07).
    2. R2 area law S ∝ L², Acs ∝ L².
    3. R3 wing loading W/S ∝ L.
    4. R4 minimum speed v ≥ √(2W/(ρSCL,max)), and vmin ∝ L½ under isometry (thompson1917-ch02-06 first half, ch02-10).
    5. R5 power required Preq = Wv/(L/D) ∝ L3.5 (ch02-06 second half).
    6. R6 power available Pavail = f p M ∝ L³ as a named assumption; with R5 a size L* exists above which flight fails (ch02-07).
    7. R7 strength Fbreak = σ Acs ∝ L², safety factor ∝ L−1. First in line.
    8. R8 Froude v ∝ √(gL) for swimming and walking (ch02-10).

    How this was done

    1. Prior art first. A logged search for flying-fish aerodynamics and glide performance before any model: OpenAlex via the OpenResearch CLI (rate-limited after one call), then Europe PMC, Crossref and Semantic Scholar. Every call is in SEARCHES.md; findings in PRIOR-ART.md. No run identifier is issued by the tool, so none is recorded. No email, name or account id went into any request.
    2. Pre-register, then read. The test's pass and fail rules were written to result.json at 18:21 UTC before any wing loading, lifting area or lift coefficient had been read; the file lists the few values seen in abstracts before that moment. One amendment at 18:26 UTC, requested by the hub before any fit ran, separates gliders from powered fliers and names the flying fish in the set.
    3. Fetch, verify, record. Candidate DOIs recalled from memory were resolved in Crossref and kept only when the title matched; one guess returned 404 and is cited nowhere. Paywalled texts are cited as abstract-only. Open copies came from PubMed Central, Zenodo, Inter-Research and the Wayback Machine.
    4. Compute from files. analysis.py reads the two CSVs and writes every number to results.json; this page reads that file. Air density 1.225 kg/m³ and g = 9.81 m/s² are the only constants.
    5. Keep the kinds apart. Measured, secondary, theory and simulation are separate labels on every row. A CFD result is a simulation and enters no verdict.

    What this does not establish

    • That Thompson's scaling law holds: both fits are underpowered by the pre-registered rules. The pooled slope of 0.54 across eight points is descriptive only; it mixes gliders and powered fliers.
    • That the flying fish's glide speeds were measured on the fish whose wing loading was measured. The speeds are family-level field figures quoted second-hand from works published between 1941 and 1994; the morphometrics are 40 to 60 g stuffed specimens of one species.
    • Anything about muscle power: no source gives the muscle mass fraction or specific power of a flying fish.
    • Anything about birds. Class Aves is held out; the exam on birds is for an assessor who built none of this.
    • Any novelty. Fish 1990 and Park and Choi 2010 already compared flying-fish wings with birds and bats; what we did not find is a pre-registered test of the (W/S)½ law on gliders, and Google Scholar was not searched.
    • That the compiled bat and hoverfly values are right: the compilation's own mass and area columns disagree with its wing-loading column for insects by a factor of 5 to 8, and its primary sources were not fetched.
