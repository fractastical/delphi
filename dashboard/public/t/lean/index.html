Can a law of biology be checked by a computer? One can.

This page reports on lean/, where DELPHI writes the mathematical laws it has extracted as theorems in Lean 4, a proof checker, and asks the checker whether each proof is complete. Every number here is read from lean.json, which lean/report.mjs writes after running the compiler. Nothing is typed by hand.

What is proved, from what

The argument, in plain words

  1. Set up a population of M gene copies (M = 2N for N diploid individuals). A new mutation is present in 1 of them. Each generation, the next M copies are drawn at random from the current ones. That is the Wright–Fisher model, and in Lean it is literally the binomial formula C(M, j) (i/M)^j (1 - i/M)^(M-j).
  2. Neutral means the average does not drift. If i copies are mutant now, the expected number next generation is exactly i. The Lean structure NeutralChain takes this as a hypothesis, and the Wright–Fisher instance proves it from the binomial formula (via Mathlib's Bernstein polynomials).
  3. Sooner or later the lineage is lost or fixed. From any in-between state there is a positive chance of finishing in one step, so the chance of still being undecided after t generations shrinks like ρt for some ρ below 1. This is unresolved_le_pow.
  4. Conservation of the mean does the rest. i = M × P(fixed by t) + (undecided mass), and the undecided mass vanishes, so P(eventual fixation) = i/M. For a single new mutant, 1/M. This is fixedBy_tendsto, with the explicit error bound in fixedBy_sub_le.
  5. Kimura's step. Mutations arrive at Mμ per generation; each fixes with probability 1/M; so substitutions happen at Mμ × 1/M = μ per generation, whatever M is. That is neutral_substitution_rate. The population size cancels, which is why a molecular clock can exist.

A proof shows the conclusion follows from the assumptions. It says nothing about whether real genomes satisfy them. That is the job of the claim tests, and the gap is deliberate.

Second theorem, in plain words: Fisher's fundamental theorem

  1. Types and fitnesses. A population has finitely many types (alleles at one locus in a haploid or clonal organism), each with a frequency p and a fitness w. Mean fitness w̄ is the frequency-weighted average of w. This is meanFitness.
  2. One generation of selection. Each type's share next generation is p × w / w̄. The Lean theorem select_isFreq checks that this is again a set of frequencies summing to one.
  3. Hold the fitnesses fixed and measure the change. That is the "partial change" of Price and Ewens: no frequency dependence, no changing environment. Then the new mean fitness minus the old equals the variance in fitness divided by the old mean. This is fisher_fundamental_theorem. Because every type breeds true in a haploid population, that variance is the additive genetic variance the claim names.
  4. Corollary. A variance is never negative, so mean fitness never falls under fixed fitnesses: mean_fitness_nondecreasing. The claim's own context says the total change can fall when fitnesses are not fixed, and nothing here says otherwise.

Third theorem, in plain words: the twofold cost of sex

  1. Two kinds of mothers. A fraction q of females reproduce without sex and have only daughters; the rest reproduce sexually and, with a 1:1 sex ratio, half their offspring are sons. Every mother has the same number of offspring. Males contribute nothing to the count.
  2. Count next generation's daughters. The asexual share becomes q·f / (q·f + (1−q)·f/2). This is asexualNext.
  3. The odds double. twofold_cost_of_sex proves that the odds of asexual to sexual females next generation are exactly twice this generation's, and the fecundity f cancels. Iterated, twofold_cost_of_sex_n gives 2n after n generations. That factor of two is the advantage sex must buy back to persist.

Did evolution have time? The flying-fish budget

Darwin (1859, ch. VI) wrote that flying-fish "might have been modified into perfectly winged animals". The fixation theorems above, extended to selection that does not oppose a mutant, bound how much substitution the Exocoetidae lineage has had since its oldest fossil.     Commands
    

    cd lean
lake exe cache get      # Mathlib, prebuilt
lake build              # compiles every module
lake env lean Delphi/Audit.lean   # prints axioms
cd .. && node lean/report.mjs    # writes t/lean/lean.json
    

    Needs elan (the Lean toolchain manager) and about 8 GB of disk for Mathlib. The exact Mathlib commit is in lean/lake-manifest.json; the toolchain is in lean/lean-toolchain.
    

    

    


    Try to break it: mutation evidence
    

    A proof nobody tried to break is not evidence. lean/mutate.mjs changes the statement of each headline theorem (or deletes a hypothesis of the model) in a copy of the module and asks the compiler to check the copy. Every mutant must be rejected; the unmodified modules must pass through the same command.

    What this does not show
    

    
 
    Not shown: that organisms obey the model
    Strict neutrality, constant population size, non-overlapping generations and independent fates of mutants are assumptions. The theorem is exact inside the model and silent outside it. The claim's proposed empirical test compares synonymous-site substitution rates with pedigree-measured mutation rates; that is a claim-test job, not a Lean job.
    
 
    Not shown: that the Lean statement is a faithful translation
    The project rule is that whether a Lean statement renders the paper's law faithfully is judged by an assessor from a different model provider than the one that wrote it. These modules were written by Claude (Anthropic). 
    
 
    Not shown: a published nanopub
    Nothing here has been signed or published. The plan is a delphi:Concept nanopub per law carrying the Lean source and a commit-pinned link, on the test registry first, with leanVerified true only because the audit above says zero sorry and standard axioms.
    
 
    Scope of the fixation theorem
    Kimura's 1962 formula covers selection (s ≠ 0) in the diffusion limit. The Lean theorem covers the neutral case only, for the exact finite Wright–Fisher chain rather than the diffusion approximation. That is the part the substitution-rate law actually needs.
