AN OPEN PORTAL FOR THE STUDY OF BIOLOGICAL FORM

Why do living things have the shapes they do?
A century of mathematical answers, tested in the open.

Delphi collects the mathematical ideas people have proposed to explain the shapes of animals and plants, from a 1917 theory that one fish becomes another by stretching a grid, to modern models of how cells use electricity to decide where a head belongs. Each idea is rebuilt as a working program, checked against real specimens, and asked the hardest question in science: can it predict a shape it has never seen?

Learn from what is known. Predict what was hidden. Score it in public.

HELD OUT—PREDICTED—OBSERVEDhidden

1 · Learn a space from measured species

Hundreds of fishes are measured at the same seventeen points. The model learns a small set of “knobs”, directions in which fish bodies actually vary. Each knob is a named deformation first drawn by hand in 1917, so every position in the space can be turned back into a shape.

2 · Hold something back and predict it

A species, or a whole moment in the past, is removed. Using only its relatives and the family tree, the model places it in the space and says how far it could be wrong. The guess is written down and sealed with a cryptographic hash before anyone looks at the answer.

3 · Reveal the answer and keep the score

The hidden measurement is opened and compared. Successes and failures are both recorded as signed statements that cannot be edited later. In the latest blind test, four of seven pre-registered claims held and three did not; all seven are published.

EVIDENCE

We publish what went wrong, not just what went right.

4 of 7 sealed predictions held

Guesses for 10,888 species were written down and sealed before anyone looked at the answers. Three of seven claims failed, and the failures are on the page.

Two independent referees

Every major model is scored by two automated reviewers that never see each other’s work. When they disagree by 37 points, the disagreement is published, not averaged away.

A limit, stated in advance

Shape prediction works well within about 50 million years and breaks down beyond that. The breakdown was predicted before the test and then found in the data.

FIVE WORDS YOU WILL SEE A LOT

A short glossary.

ModelA set of rules written as mathematics that, given a few numbers, draws a shape. Here, every model is also a program you can run.
PredictionA statement about a shape made before anyone has measured it, with a stated range of how wrong it might be.
Held outA measurement deliberately hidden from the model so that it can be used to test the model afterwards.
AuditA scored check, by two independent reviewers, that a program really does what its source paper says and really does match specimens.
Signed recordA small, machine-readable statement of a result, cryptographically signed and published so it can be cited and can never be quietly changed. The technical name is a nanopublication.

EXPLORE

One workbench, four instruments, and a way to run real computations.

The workbench runs entirely in your browser; nothing is installed and nothing is sent anywhere. Each instrument below is explained first in plain terms and then in the language a specialist would use.

01 · PREDICT

Predict a body plan at any point in evolutionary time.

In plain terms: choose a group of fishes and slide back through time; the model draws the body shape it expects their ancestor to have had, with a band showing how uncertain it is. For specialists: shape is encoded as seven linearised Thompson grid modes or the full landmark space, a Pagel-λ process is conditioned on a dated tree, and the lineage state is returned with its 95% envelope.

  • Horizon banner says whether you are inside the 50 Myr range where skill was demonstrated blind
  • Skill decomposition: consensus, nearest relative, tree conditioning, full space
  • Every prediction states the fossil that would falsify it
02 · GENERATE

Six classic models of form, running live.

In plain terms: each card is a famous idea about how a shape comes to be, with a few dials you can turn and a picture that responds. For specialists: Thompson coordinate grids, Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion, Raup shell coiling in 3D, golden-angle phyllotaxis, Rule 30 and Rule 90 shell pigment, and a bioelectric flatworm that regrows two heads when gap junctions are blocked.

  • Audit score and both referee ratings on every card
  • Save any state to the experiment notebook and compare runs
  • Export parameters as JSON or the visualisation as PNG
03 · FIT

Put a 1917 idea to the test on real museum outlines.

In plain terms: D’Arcy Thompson claimed one fish could be turned into a related one by bending a grid. For thirty years computers said he was wrong. Here you can see why the computers were measuring the wrong thing. For specialists: his six species pairs fitted two ways, to 17 skeletal landmarks and to 600-point outlines, with mismatch, a box-stretch null, and the minimum Jacobian showing the grid never folds.

  • Silhouette mismatch and overlap for three methods
  • Homologous fin-tip accuracy: box stretch vs curved grid
  • Schematic warp updates with your choice
04 · REGISTRY

Fourteen checked models, each with its own card.

In plain terms: a card tells you what a model needs, what it produces, how well it matched real specimens, and what it still gets wrong. For specialists: both referee scores, the machine-checked laws, audit history and open limits; three further scripts are listed as not yet audited.

For specialists: seven run types map to repository entry points; each run exposes state, a streaming log, a result summary, artifacts and the trusty URI of any nanopublication it signed.

WHY YOU CAN TRUST IT

Built so that nothing has to be taken on faith.

Inputs are locked

Every dataset carries a digital fingerprint. If a file has been altered, even by one byte, the run refuses to start.

Rules are proved, not assumed

The core laws of each model are written as theorems and checked by a proof-checking program in about a second, then tested on every record.

Results are signed, never overwritten

Scores and measurements are published as signed statements with permanent addresses. A new result points to the old one; neither can be quietly changed.

VERIFY IT YOURSELF

Three ways to check a Delphi number without trusting Delphi.

Resolve the nanopublication

Every score carries a trusty URI. Resolve it on the public network, check the RSA signature with @nanopub/sign, and read the measurement graph that produced the number.

Rebuild the proofs

Clone the repository and run lake build. The 25 laws check in about a second and the build fails on any sorry.

Re-run the audit

Queue a MorphoEval audit from Live runs. A new pass is signed and chained to the previous one; if your referee scores it lower, that record stands too.

Authentication

Bearer tokens in the Authorization header. Tokens are scoped: runs:read, runs:write, publish (allows the backend to sign and publish nanopublications under the token owner’s key). The workspace stores the token in this browser only and never forwards it anywhere except the configured base URL.

Run lifecycle and events

queued→running→succeeded/failed/cancelled

Events are server-sent. Each event is a JSON object with a monotonic seq; the workspace reconnects with ?since=seq. In live mode the workspace falls back to polling GET /v1/runs/{id} every two seconds when EventSource is unavailable.

event: log data: {"seq":14,"t":"18:04:19","level":"info","msg":"Chamfer fit converged in 212 iterations (loss 0.0184)"} event: progress data: {"seq":15,"pct":72,"step":"Computing det DΦ on the body grid"} event: state data: {"seq":21,"status":"succeeded","result":{"mismatch_pct":28.5,"iou":0.749,"min_det":0.714}}

Artifacts and nanopublications

A succeeded run lists artifacts by name with a content type and byte size. Audits and predictions also return nanopubs[]: each entry has the trusty URI, the signing key fingerprint and, when the run supersedes an earlier pass, the URI it supersedes. The workspace shows these verbatim and never alters them.

Webhooks

Optional. Register a URL in Integration; the backend posts the run object on every state change with an X-Delphi-Signature HMAC header. Use this to mirror run results into a lab notebook or a CI pipeline.

THE PROJECT

Life uses only a few forms.
The space of possible forms is vast.

Delphi is an open project to collect, rebuild and test the mathematics of biological form. Every model that passes its checks is turned into a prediction anyone can try to prove wrong. The cube shows one classic example: every shell shape that could exist sits at its corners, and the small pink cloud is where real shells actually live.

Delphi is a workbench over audited reproductions of form-generating models, each scored against SHA-256-locked data and formalised in Lean 4 before it is allowed to predict. The cube is Raup’s (W, D, T) parameter space: corner shells sample its extremes; the point cloud is the occupied region of measured molluscs.

What Delphi commits to

Three promises, one loop

Reproduce

One self-contained program per paper, from Thompson’s 1917 grids to bioelectric regeneration, that grows the form, draws it and checks itself.

Each script reproduces the governing system or decodes the authors’ formal encoding, with deterministic self-tests and vendored, SHA-256-pinned data.

Verify

Two referees from different companies score every model against photographed specimens; each defect becomes a fix; every score is signed and can never be quietly edited.

A 12-step loop: Lean 4 invariants (0 sorry), a 7-dimension rubric, independent measurement, RSA-2048-signed nanopublications chained by npx:supersedes.

Predict

A verified model earns the right to predict shapes nobody has measured, with error bars that were tested blind on 10,888 species before the data was opened.

Pagel-λ conditioning on a dated tree in 7 Thompson modes or the full 30-D space; pre-registered, hashed predictions scored against FishShapes v1.

Open problems Delphi has worked

Resolved: a published paradox or confound explained with a quantified result. Proved: a theorem machine-checked in Lean 4 or verified exhaustively on a stated domain. Partial and open: the project’s own statement of what remains. Every tile links to the workspace view where you can see it.

Where the skill comes from at this horizon

lower error is better

Most of what the model knows comes from the nearest measured relative. Conditioning on the whole tree adds a little; the a-priori Thompson modes trade accuracy for seven knobs with names.

Median held-out Procrustes RMS by predictor in this horizon bin (2,229 leave-out predictions). In the pre-registered FishShapes test the tree-conditioned predictor beat nearest-relative copying by only 4% on BDmax/SL; that hypothesis did not hold.

How this was predicted

Measure the relatives. 743 reef-fish species were digitised at 17 skeletal points and placed on a dated evolutionary tree.
Describe each shape with a few knobs. Either the full set of landmark coordinates, or seven of Thompson’s named grid deformations (deepen, shear, fan, bow, two coaxial bends).
Learn how shape drifts with time. The data, not an assumption, picked the rule: close relatives resemble each other almost in proportion to shared history (Pagel λ = 0.96).
Read off the lineage. The predicted state is the best estimate given every measured relative, with an interval whose width grows with distance in time.
Data. 743 Indo-Pacific species, 17 landmarks (Claverie & Wainwright 2014), on the Rabosky et al. (2018) megaphylogeny.
Encoding. Y = M₀ + Bz, with B either the full tangent-space basis or 7 Thompson modes linearised at the Procrustes consensus and orthogonalised against rigid motions. Linearity in landmarks is what makes Gaussian conditioning on the tree legitimate.
Process. WN, BM, Pagel-λ, OU and EB fitted by phylogenetic GLS; λ = 0.959 wins with ΔAICc > 2,400. OU half-life 78–91 Myr (no detectable optimum); EB collapses to BM.
Prediction. Gaussian conditional expectation of the lineage state at age τ given tips, with a 7-D predictive ellipsoid; coverage calibrated on 2,229 held-out predictions.

What would falsify this

Registered in THOMPSON_PHYLOGENETIC_PREDICTIONS.md and as signed nanopublications. Head and predorsal proportions for lineage trajectories are taken from the family value; the trajectory itself is interpolated between registered points at 0, 10, 25, 50, 75 and 100 Ma.

Thompson’s coordinate transformations, 1917

Audit 83.7
Grid folds: 0 of 0 cellsdet DΦ > 0 everywhere means the map is a diffeomorphism on the body.A fold would mean tissue passing through itself, which no growth field can do.

What this model is

Inputone outline, 5 to 8 numbers
→
Rulebend the whole grid at once
→
Outputa related species’ outline

Nature does not redesign every bone when a new species appears; it turns a few global growth knobs that stretch, slant or curve the whole body together. Shear turns a hatchetfish into its deep-bodied cousin; coaxial circles turn a parrotfish into an angelfish; the radial flare turns a porcupinefish into an ocean sunfish. The fish here is schematic.

Four coordinate families composed with the uniform affine component: shear x′ = u + gv, y′ = dv; bilinear fan y″ = y′(1 + f x′); Möbius coaxial z‴ = z″/(c z″ + 1); hyperbolic flare x⁗ = x‴ + 0.4 r y‴², y⁗ = y‴ er x‴. The readout counts grid cells with det DΦ ≤ 0.

What was learned. Fitted to full 600-point outlines, these grids recover all six of Thompson’s 1917 pairs without folding; fitted to the standard 17 skeletal points, with nine in the head, they fail. See Fit to specimens.

What was learned. On 600-semilandmark outlines, 8p grids are 100% diffeomorphic on 6/6 pairs where curve TPS folds on 6/6; across 791 species they capture 92% of the optimal 8p relative-warp variance with no trained eigenvector. Lean: thompson_unit_shear_area_preserving, tps_kernel_zero_at_origin.

An activator makes more of itself; an inhibitor spreads faster and shuts it down. Where the inhibitor outruns the activator, pattern appears on its own. Angelfish stripes insert new lines as the body grows exactly this way. Slide the diffusion ratio to 1 and the pattern dies: equal diffusion cannot pattern anything.

Gray–Scott: ∂tu = Du∇²u − uv² + F(1 − u); ∂tv = Dv∇²v + uv² − (F + k)v. The homogeneous state (1, 0) is an equilibrium; differential diffusion above the critical ratio dc = 3.9271 drives det Jk < 0 at some wavenumber.

What was learned. The first audit scored this model 38 out of 100: it lacked the original dispersion analysis, had no phase diagram and no comparison to a real animal. One remediation pass fixed all five defects and brought it to 85.5.

What was learned. Rulesweep a growing tube round an axis

Almost every snail, nautilus and ammonite is the same thing: a tube that widens as it coils. Three numbers describe it: how fast the whorl expands, how far it sits from the axis, and how much it slides down the axis per turn. Most of the space of possible shells has no animal in it, which is itself a finding about what life can build.

Generating curve along a logarithmic spiral: whorl radius ×W per revolution, inner edge at D·R, translation T per whorl. Real shells occupy a small structured region of (W, D, T). Lean: raup_whorl_expansion_recovery, raup_translation_rate_recovery (W and T recoverable from consecutive whorls).

Bioelectric pattern memory: Lobo & Levin 2013, Cervera, Levin & Mafé 2021

Audit 71.5 and 58.1
Outcome: one head, one tail
Try: coupling to 0.1, then cut.

What this model is

Inputa cut, and how well cells talk
→
Rulethe head sends a signal through gap junctions
→
Outputone head, or two

Every cell holds a resting voltage, and neighbouring cells share it through gap junctions. The existing head sends a signal down the body that tells the far end to become a tail. Block the junctions, cut, and it regrows a second head. The databases this project decodes record hundreds of such cut-and-block experiments as formal graphs.

Bistable field si with head bias β, coupled to an inhibitor I sourced by head tissue and carried by gap-junction conductance G: ṡ = s − s³ + β − γI; İ = G·D∇²I + σ[head] − μI. Two attractors, one anatomy each. Lean: cervera_equipotential_zero_current, cervera_bell_boltzmann_symmetric.

How to read this

For thirty years the computer verdict on Thompson was negative, and the reason was the ruler, not the idea. Fitting his grid to nine head points and eight fin bases lets the head drag the sheet the wrong way for the rest of the body; the warped fish can come out worse than no warp at all. Fit the same grid to the whole outline, anchored only at snout and tail base, and every one of the six 1917 pairs is recovered with no folding.

17-landmark fits of interfamily pairs degrade |ΩAΔΩB| relative to the 4p similarity baseline; direct 600-semilandmark Chamfer fits of the same 5–8p families reduce it on 6/6 pairs with det DΦ > 0 everywhere and bidirectional invertibility, where 600-point curve TPS folds on 6/6 (min det −6.76) and EFD defines no interior map.

The second chart guards against a cheat: on an unlabelled silhouette a plain box stretch explains most of the overlap. Force the grid to map fin tip to fin tip and the box fails, while Thompson’s curves jump by 11 to 48 points.

Unlabelled Chamfer matching lets a 4p orthotropic box carry 81–100% of the silhouette gain on 5/6 pairs; on homologous perimeter + fin-apex landmarks (12 points, no skull) the box fails (2.4% on Scarus → Pomacanthus) and the 1917 warps gain +11 to +48 points.

A model earns the right to predict only after it is audited

Every model goes through the same twelve steps before anything it produces is treated as a prediction. Two independent referees, language models from different companies, score it; every defect they find becomes a fix; the scores are signed and published where nobody, including the authors, can quietly edit them.

The loop separates three claims usually conflated: that the code implements the paper, that the output matches the organism, and that someone else can reproduce it. Each is scored on a weighted rubric, hashed, serialised as RDF nanopublications and signed with RSA-2048; a remediated pass supersedes rather than overwrites its predecessor.

What is still open

The horizon

Interval coverage falls from 93% to 65% past 50 Myr, and distant teleosts come out too deep-bodied because the training set is reef fish. Beyond the horizon the right statement is “degrades to a clade-wide prior”.

Most of the skill is the nearest relative

Conditioning on the whole tree beat copying the closest training species by only 4% on body depth. The registered hypothesis that it would do better did not hold.

Heads are not predicted

Sister species on the same reef rewire jaws, snouts and eyes 3.3 to 9.3 times faster than their bodies. The craniofacial module is modelled as a separate residual and left out of the registered predictions.

Fossils are a null so far

Sixteen published ratios on ten Eocene fossils from Monte Bolca sit near the stasis null. Eoplatax falsified the one sharp stem-state prediction: the batfish disc was already in place 50 Myr ago.

Bioelectric models score lowest

Cervera–Levin–Mafé audits at 58.1 and coral flow at 55.7. Scores are referee ratings; several flagship numbers fell on re-audit when earlier versions rested on schematic figures or circular validation.
