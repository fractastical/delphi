## Per-criterion justifications and gaps

### Problem significance & complexity

**Gemini 4 Argon: 8.** Identifying, formalizing, and empirically testing candidate laws of biology across diverse taxa and scales is a genuinely difficult, open-ended, and scientifically consequential problem (`repo/README.md`, `captured/mcp-overview.json`). The snapshot demonstrates why this separation of mathematical proof from biological reality matters: across 1,702 extracted claims (including 162 with formal mathematical statements and 63 established theorems), only 2 mathematical claims are currently supported by data measured on living organisms (`captured/mcp-overview.json`, `captured/mcp-tests-summary.json`). Grounding historical and modern theoretical claims (Darwin, Thompson, Wolfram, Levin, Aristotle, and mathematical biology) against 1,036 resolved taxa and 1,372 provenanced datasets (`repo/test-data/organisms/README.md`, `captured/mcp-organisms.json`) addresses a real epistemic bottleneck in theoretical and comparative biology, though much of the current corpus still consists of historical book claims rather than modern open frontier hypotheses.

- Expand empirical testing on the modern biological frontier (`claim-sources/levin-papers/`, where only 2 of 310 claims are tested in `captured/mcp-tests-summary.json`, and `claim-sources/math-bio-claims/`, where 0 of 21 are data-tested) so the problem demonstrated is not predominantly historical 19th/20th-century claims.
- Ground claim entities to external biological identifiers (HGNC, GO, NCBITaxon) as proposed in issue #34 so cross-graph mechanistic tests become systematic rather than ad-hoc.

**GPT-5.6 Sol: 9.** The project tackles a genuinely hard and consequential problem: converting broad biological laws into source-traceable, formalizable and empirically testable claims across organisms. The snapshot shows the difficulty rather than merely asserting it: evidence/captured/mcp-overview.json reports 1,702 graph claims, 162 mathematical claims, only 63 theorems, only 2 mathematical claims supported by organism data, and 1,508 claims neither proved nor tested; evidence/captured/mcp-organisms.json further shows 1,036 matched taxa but only 2 with bioelectric coverage. This is an ambitious open scientific-infrastructure problem with clear unresolved scientific content, although the pack does not show that solving the whole claim inventory would directly change a working scientist's decisions.

- Show two or three concrete decisions by working biologists that the graph changes, rather than only the size of the unresolved inventory.
- Prioritize a narrow law family with a stated scientific consequence if confirmed or refuted across organisms.

**Grok 4.7: 8.** The problem the snapshot actually works is hard and still open: which candidate biological laws, stated so a machine can cite them, survive a proof check and a data check, with those two axes kept apart. repo/README.md and repo/AGENTS.md define a theorem as a math claim that is Lean-checked or proved in the literature, and say a data verdict never promotes a claim. captured/mcp-overview.json (claim graph built 2026-10-31 20:44) counts 1702 claims, 162 with math, 63 theorems (47 Lean, 16 literature), 2 math claims supported by organism measurements, 133 data-tested (78 supports, 25 mixed, 21 refutes, 9 inconclusive; 66 on organisms), and 1508 neither proved nor tested.
