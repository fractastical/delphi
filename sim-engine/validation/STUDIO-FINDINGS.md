# Design Studio validation: findings and worst defects

Independent validation thread (a software agent acting as the skeptic; it built no studio module).
Tests fixed in `STUDIO-PREREGISTRATION.md` (sha256 `f2523da8…a66d`, hashed 2026-11-01T02:30:37Z, before any
scenario module existed). One post-results amendment, labelled as such, is in `STUDIO-AMENDMENTS.md`.
Live numbers are in `studio-results.json` / `STUDIO-RESULTS.md`, regenerated by `studio-harness.mjs`. The
numbers below are from the run at about 02:48 UTC, after the D0 fix (all ten scenarios
plus `glide` loaded). **Re-check `STUDIO-RESULTS.md` for the current run.**

## The one honest sentence

**Everything the studio got "right" about the wild-type zebrafish it got right by reading the answer back:
of the six numeric wild-type tests that landed in band, five are interpolations or read-backs of the very
papers they were scored against. The two predictions that were not read back missed (C-start speed, larval
sleep). The negative and optimizer controls all held, but the glide model gives a 0.5 mg zebrafish larva a
42.8 m glide and labels it as surviving.**

## Worst defects, most serious first

**D0. The whole studio was down for a few minutes around 02:45 UTC (integration regression; reported to the hub, fixed by the owner by 02:47, and the verdicts above re-run afterwards).**
`lib/data-node.mjs` changed `bundle.manifests[name]` from an array to an object `{file, objects, …}`;
`lib/organism.mjs` `partsOf()` still iterates it as an array, so `resolveState` throws "object is not
iterable" for every organism, and every scenario, the optimizer and the harness fail with it. Not edited
here (other agents own both files).

**D1. Larval and designed zebrafish "glide" tens of metres and are labelled as surviving**
(`scenarios/glide.mjs`, `wind.mjs`, `altitude.mjs`). Every organism gets the flying-fish field launch speed
of 15 m/s as its default. A 0.54 mg zebrafish larva then gets `glide_range_m` 42.8 m with
`survives: true`, and 23.4 m in a headwind (`wind.mjs`); winged-zebrafish gets 39.6 m. The only guard is
`validity.inRange = false` with a note. The adult zebrafish passes N1 only because v_min (17 m/s) exceeds
the 15 m/s default. Even so, it is still handed a 4.37 m `glide_range_m` for a glide its own `survives`
flag says cannot happen. A number for an impossible event should be null, not a metric of kind
`derived`.

**D2. The studio's glide number is not covered by the proved Lean law** (`glide.mjs`; N5 fail).
`glide_range_le` bounds a steady unpowered glide from height h by h·(L/D)max/(1−δ), which is 4.37 m from
1 m. `glide_range_m` adds a level "kinetic-energy" phase, (L/D)(v0² − v_min²)/2g, and that phase supplies
28.5 of the flying fish's 32.9 m. Physically that phase may be defensible, but it lies outside the
theorem's hypotheses, and the UI shows it next to Lean verdicts as if they were checked together. Also,
that phase is driven by the assumed 15 m/s launch speed, taken from the same field observations
(Edgerton & Breder via Makiguchi 2013) that the module's own `validatedAgainst` then scores as a "PASS"
against 50 m. That is partly circular.

**D3. Adult C-start speed is a borrowed pike lower bound, half the measured zebrafish value**
(`predator.mjs`; W8 fail). `peak_escape_speed_m_s` = 0.41 m/s (11 BL/s, from Schuster 2023's pike quote).
The module says "no fetched adult-zebrafish C-start speed". Danos & Lauder 2012 (J Exp Biol 215:1854,
doi:10.1242/jeb.068957; readable via its Wayback copy) give a mean maximum Reynolds number of 27,800 for
33 mm zebrafish, which is about 0.84 m/s (25 TL/s). The halved speed feeds
`critical_predator_speed_ratio` and `aerial_escape_available`.

**D4. Five wild-type "validations" are read-backs (amendment A1).** These are `temperature.mjs`
(CTmax/CTmin interpolated from Cortemeglia & Beitinger 2005, labelled `derived`, while its own model card
calls the same numbers "the calibration data"), `current.mjs` (Plaut 2000's 15.5 SL/s × SL) and
`sleep.mjs` (Yokogawa 2007 quoted). They should be labelled `calibrated` per the project's own rule, and
never shown as a validated result. Credit where due: `temperature.mjs` carries real out-of-sample checks
in its model card (Morgan 2018, 40.97 °C, predicted 41.2; Andreassen 2022).

**D5. The optimizer never scores the start design** (`tools/studio.mjs optimize`). Restarts begin at
random points in the variable box. So on an objective the start already maximises (O1: CTmax, which no
fin edit changes), it returns a randomly re-shaped fish (zebrafish pectoral span/SL 0.47, area/SL² 0.008)
as `status: "ok"`, with no comparison to the start. It reported no false improvement, so O1 passes. But a
user would read "optimised design" for a random morphology. It also reports no improvement relative to
the start at all, and a run could in principle return something worse than the start with status ok.

**D6. `survives` means different things in different scenarios.** In `altitude.mjs` the zebrafish adult
gets `survives: true` while `limiting` says "launch airspeed 15 m/s is below v_min 19.03 m/s". There
`survives` tracks water hypoxia, whereas in `glide.mjs` it means lift can carry the weight. The optimizer
uses `survives` as a hard constraint across scenarios.

**D7. Larval vs adult sleep compares two different definitions** (`sleep.mjs`; W7 fail). The larval
figure (Choudhary 2026, speed < 0.5 mm/s for ≥ 1 min: 83.9 % of night at 28 °C, 87.3 % after the
temperature factor) and Sorribes 2013's larval 58.2 % disagree by 25 points. The adult figure uses a
6-second criterion. The pre-registered fail stands. The sign (larva > adult) is robust, because the
stricter larval criterion should give less sleep, not more.

**D8. One citation could not be verified.** `predator.mjs` cites Burgess & Granato 2007, *"Sensorimotor
gating in larval zebrafish"*, for an ADULT startle latency ("modal latency to startle being 8 ms",
"full text read"). Europe PMC does not serve that full text (PMC6672105), the publisher returns 403, and
the abstract does not mention adults or 8 ms. The claim may well be in a figure inset, but it is
unverifiable here, and the paper's title is about larvae.

**D9. Minor.** `digestion.mjs` has no gut-passage metric (W10). The parser turns "a fish at 10,000 m
depth" into a spec with `start_from: []`. `glide.mjs` labels g = 9.81 as `assumed`, which drags the
weakest kind of every glide metric down to `assumed`. Freshwater density 998.2 kg/m³ (`pressure.mjs`) and
the ground-effect breakpoints 0.33 / 0.86 / 0.97 (`glide.mjs`) have no source within three lines.

## Source audit (part 3)

Code literals only: 159 lines carry constants across the 11 modules. 25 have a URL/DOI/PMID nearby, 89
cite by author-year or repository file only, and 45 have neither. Most of the unsourced ones are
defaults and validity bounds; the substantive ones are listed under D9. Spot checks by real GET of the
cited paper: 10 confirmed, 1 unverifiable (D8), 1 not fetchable through Europe PMC/Crossref (Makiguchi
2013's "50 m", which the physics thread read from the publisher PDF). Confirmed: Widrick 2018 (15.5,
15 %), McKee & McHenry 2020 (10.3–15.8°, 740–780 ms), Schuster 2023 (11 BL/s, 24 BL/s), Choudhary 2026
(19.6 %, 83.9 %), Ripley 2022 (Q10 1.39, 1.15), Yancey 2014 (261, 991, 8,200 m), Lauridsen 2022 (5 %
bladder, 5 % denser), Durnford 2026 (58.48 ml/min/kg). No mis-sourced constant was found among those
checked.

## What this does not establish

The bands are wide on purpose, because the targets disagree among themselves. A pass here is a sanity
check, not validation. The genotype side, the morphogen tool and the UI were not tested. No Aves data was
used.
